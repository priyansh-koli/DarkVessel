Fusion

What it is: the furthest a declared AIS position may sit from a radar detection and still be paired with it, one declaration per detection.
Use: tighten it and loosely placed reports stop explaining their vessels, so they turn dark; loosen it and another vessel's report can start hiding a dark one.
On the radar: the dashed ring round each AIS declaration.
200 m

What it is: how old a vessel's nearest AIS report may be when no pair of reports brackets the acquisition. Bracketed tracks are interpolated whatever the gap.
Use: lengthen it to let stale reports into the search. Here a trawler last reported 14 min before the pass, and another vessel 25 min before, near the dark contact.

10 min

Beyond this, a lone report is too stale to place the vessel at all.

Detector

What it is: the minimum normalised pixel brightness, 0 to 1, the stand-in detector counts as a target. Each connected bright blob is one detection.
Use: lower it to pick up faint, small craft; raise it to keep only strong returns. Targets here range from 0.35 to 0.90.
On the radar: contacts appear or disappear.
0.50

The stand-in detector flags pixels at or above this, one detection per blob.

Structure register

Recurrence needs a multi-scene archive. On a single scene you can stand in for one by registering known fixed positions — a turbine list, say — and watch dark detections that sit on them reclassify.

What it is: the radius round each registered fixed position (a turbine, a platform) inside which a dark detection counts as that structure.
Use: register a position by clicking the scene, then widen this until the dark contact is reclassified. Matched vessels are never reclassified.
On the radar: the ring round each registered cross.
100 m
